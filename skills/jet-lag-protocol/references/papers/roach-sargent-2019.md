# Roach, Sargent 2019

*Interventions to minimize jet lag after westward and eastward flight.* Frontiers in Physiology 10:927. ([open access](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2019.00927/full))

**The current synthesis review of jet-lag interventions, replacing Eastman & Burgess 2009 as the "most current" reference.**

## Methods (one line)
Comprehensive narrative review of 2009–2019 literature on pre-flight, in-flight, and post-arrival jet-lag interventions, with quantitative summary tables of phase-shift magnitudes by intervention type.

## Numbers used by the algorithm

### Adaptation rates with full intervention (re-confirmed from Eastman & Burgess 2009)

| Direction | Rate | Mechanism cited |
|---|---|---|
| Advance (eastward) | 1.0 h/day | Light + 0.5–3 mg melatonin + sleep schedule |
| Delay (westward) | 1.5 h/day | Light + sleep schedule (melatonin role contested) |

Confirms the algorithm's R3 ceilings as still current.

### Adaptation rates without intervention (real-world flights)

| Direction | Rate |
|---|---|
| Advance | ~57 min/day |
| Delay | ~92 min/day |

### Pre-flight protocols

Roach & Sargent emphasize:

1. **Wake-time light is the single highest-leverage intervention.** Restated multiple times: morning bright light (≥5,000 lux outdoor or 2,500 lux indoor box) for 30+ min on each pre-flight day is necessary for the rate ceiling to be reached. **This grounds the algorithm's R8.3 wake-anchor light rule with the most current citation.**

2. **Pre-flight sleep schedule shifts of >1 h/day produce diminishing returns** because the circadian system can't keep up with behavioral changes faster than ~1 h/day. Aggressive shifts (>1.5 h/day) often trigger acute insomnia and sleep debt with no faster phase shift. **Justifies the algorithm's R3 firm-ceiling at 1.5 h/day for delays, no higher.**

3. **Pre-flight protocols for short trips (<3 days at destination) underperform.** The cost of pre-shifting plus partial re-adaptation back exceeds the benefit. **Justifies R11 (short-trip skip).**

### Recovery prescriptions

- **Wake-time light at home time after arrival is the dominant recovery intervention**, more important than pre-flight prep
- Caffeine: explicitly cited as alertness-only, **not phase-shifting**, in the jet-lag context. Burke 2015's small phase effect (40 min delay from 200 mg) is acknowledged as too small to matter for protocol design
- Melatonin recovery dose for advance jet lag: 0.5–3 mg at destination bedtime for 2–3 nights post-arrival, then taper

### Direction asymmetry

Roach & Sargent attribute the advance/delay asymmetry to:
1. Human τ ≈ 24.18 h (Czeisler et al. 1999, *Science*) — slightly long → easier to delay than advance
2. Phase-response curve asymmetry — delay zone (peak −5h pre-CBT_min) has larger amplitude than advance zone (peak +3h post-CBT_min) for light pulses
3. Practical adherence — delay protocols are easier to maintain alongside normal life (eat dinner later, go out later) vs advance protocols (wake earlier on weekdays)

### What's new vs Eastman & Burgess 2009

- Stronger evidence base for **wake-time light dominance** (10+ new RCTs)
- Recommended **3-day pre-flight protocol** for advances >7h (was less prescriptive in 2009)
- Acknowledgment that **individual variability** in adaptation rate is large — some users genuinely shift faster, some slower
- More skeptical view of caffeine's phase-shifting role
- New emphasis on **post-arrival recovery > pre-flight prep** (was reverse in 2009)

## Practical implications encoded in algorithm

- **Confirms current R3 ceilings** (1.0 advance, 1.5 delay)
- **Strengthens R8.3 wake-anchor rule** — Roach & Sargent's emphasis on wake-time light is the strongest current literature support
- **Updates the recovery prescription** — recovery should dominate, not pre-flight prep, in the protocol's "leverage moments" weighting
- **Justifies R11 short-trip skip** with current evidence

## Caveats

- Narrative review (no formal meta-analysis); inclusion criteria not strict
- Author group has a pre-flight-protocol research focus — the slight bias toward "pre-flight protocols are useful but limited" is consistent with their own published work
- Most cited evidence is from <50-subject lab studies; field-validation in actual jet-lagged travelers is still scarce

## Algorithm dependence

- **R3 (adaptation rate)** — citation upgrade from Eastman & Burgess 2009 to Roach & Sargent 2019 as "current"
- **R8.3 (wake-anchor light)** — Roach & Sargent is now the primary citation
- **R11 (short-trip skip)** — Roach & Sargent provides 2009–2019 evidence base for the skip
- **Recovery prescription weighting** — should be elevated in the algorithm's "leverage moments" output, given Roach & Sargent's emphasis
